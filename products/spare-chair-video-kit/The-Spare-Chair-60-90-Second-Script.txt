Every Sunday morning, Walter arrived before anyone else at the little church on Maple Street. He unlocked the doors, turned on the lights, straightened the rows, and then did one unusual thing.

He placed one extra chair at the end of the front row.

No one ever sat there.

One day, a young volunteer asked, “Walter, why do you keep saving that seat?”

Walter smiled and said, “That chair is for the person who almost didn’t come. The one sitting in the parking lot, wondering if they are too broken, too ashamed, or too far gone. I want them to know we were expecting them.”

Weeks passed. The chair stayed empty.

Then, on a rainy Sunday, a young man stepped through the back door, soaked, exhausted, and ready to leave before anyone noticed him.

Walter saw him first. He walked over, smiled, and pointed toward the front.

“We saved you a seat,” he said.

The young man sat down and cried through the songs. After the service, he whispered, “I almost didn’t come today.”

Walter nodded. “I know.”

Years later, that young man became a pastor. And every Sunday, before anyone arrived, he set out one extra chair.

Because grace had once made room for him.
